HOA COLLECTION POLICY: SAMPLE OUTLINE
Alloy Growth Partners · alloygp.co/resources/collections/collection-policy

This outline provides general operational education, not legal advice.
Requirements vary by jurisdiction and association.

NOT A LEGAL TEMPLATE. This outline shows structure only. Terms, amounts and
notice periods depend on the association's governing documents and on state
law, which varies and changes. The association's attorney must review the
final policy before the board adopts it. Every [bracket] is a decision for the
board and the attorney.

1. PURPOSE AND AUTHORITY
   Cite the declaration and bylaw sections and the state statute that
   authorize the policy.

2. ASSESSMENTS AND DUE DATES
   Regular and special assessments. Due date. Grace period [X days].

3. DELINQUENCY
   Date an account is delinquent. Late charge [$ or %]. Interest [rate].
   Each within what the governing documents and state law allow.

4. NOTICE SEQUENCE
   Reminder at [X days]. Late notice at [X days]. Any statutory notice, with
   its required content and delivery method (certified mail where required).

5. PAYMENT PLANS
   When payment plans must be offered, when the board may offer them, and the
   applicable terms [length, minimum payment]. Who approves. What ends a plan.

6. APPLICATION OF PAYMENTS
   Order in which a payment is applied, per statute where one applies
   (some states require assessments first).

7. INTERMEDIATE (PRE-LEGAL) STAGE, IF THE BOARD ADOPTS ONE
   Trigger [X days past due, with the whole sequence reviewed by counsel against
   applicable deadlines]. Who runs it [internal team / vendor / counsel's pre-lien
   cadence]. Window [X days]. Scope statement: the pre-legal stage excludes legal
   advice, lien recording, lawsuits and foreclosure; accounts requiring those
   steps receive legal review and follow applicable board-approval requirements.
   Account verification against the ledger (not a substitute for required
   debt-validation notices or dispute procedures); follow-up pauses when
   applicable law requires it. Who pays which charges. Board approval or a
   management agreement alone does not establish that a charge may be added to
   a homeowner's balance: counsel confirms the legal basis, applicable limits and
   required notices for each charge. Return to the board with a full contact
   history.

8. ATTORNEY REFERRAL
   Trigger, and the circumstances that require earlier referral (disputes,
   bankruptcy, deadlines). Board decision. Contents of the file sent to counsel.
   Which costs are added to the owner's balance, with the legal basis for each
   confirmed by counsel.

9. LIENS, SUITS AND FORECLOSURE
   Reference to the attorney's process and the statutory requirements. Legal
   review and applicable board-approval requirements at each step.

10. RECORDS AND REPORTING
    Monthly report contents (aging by bucket, stage of each account,
    movement, next action and date). Retention of notices and contact logs.

11. ADOPTION AND REVIEW
    Resolution date. Annual review date, and review on any change in state law.
